    Welcome to Atelier

    On this page
    1. What you can do
    2. What makes Atelier different
    3. Where to start
    4. A short glossary

    Atelier lets you build your website yourself, seeing it exactly as it will be published. You put building blocks in place (a heading, a photo, a button, a map…), write your texts straight onto the page, choose your colours, and Atelier produces a real website, ready to go online.

    No programming needed. No account to create. Atelier opens in your browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari), on a computer.

    What you can do

    • Put your pages together with the mouse: you drag blocks onto the page and arrange them in columns.
    • Write straight onto the page: double-click a text and start typing, just as in a word processor.
    • Choose the look of the whole site in one place: colours, fonts, rounded corners, text sizes.
    • Check the site on a phone: one button shows the page as it looks on a smartphone, and you can adjust each element for mobile.
    • Start from ready-made sections: a big welcome heading, features, an FAQ, a contact section, a gallery…
    • Reuse a block on several pages: change it once and every page follows.
    • Keep an events calendar, news or a directory: you fill in one form per item, and Atelier creates the lists and a page for each one.
    • Publish your site in several languages, with a table showing everything that still needs translating.
    • Look after your place in Google: a score for each page and practical advice.
    • Receive messages through a contact form.
    • Put the site online with your hosting provider, in one click.
    • Work with others on the same site, or have it reviewed and commented on without any risk of breaking it.
    • Go back at any time.

    What makes Atelier different

    No subscription, no account. Atelier needs no sign-up: you open the editor and get to work.

    Your site and your data stay yours. Your project is saved automatically on your computer, in your browser. At any time you can save it to a file (File → Save project (.json)) to keep it, copy it to another computer or hand it over to someone. The site Atelier produces is made of simple pages (HTML) that you can host wherever you like.

    Several languages, including Japanese. A site can exist in French, Dutch, German, English and Japanese. The texts Atelier adds to the site itself (“Menu”, the video buttons, the dates in your events…) are written in the language of each page. The editor itself speaks French, English or Japanese.

    An AI that runs on your computer, without sending anything. If your browser allows it (a recent Chrome on a computer), Atelier uses the AI built into Chrome to rewrite a text, correct mistakes, describe an image, suggest a Google description or translate. Your texts and images never leave your computer. It is optional: everything else works without AI.

    Working together without a central server. You invite someone with a link: your two browsers talk to each other directly, in encrypted form, and each of you sees the other’s changes live. A reviewer can comment without being able to change anything.

    Publish with your hosting provider, OVH for example. Atelier prepares a small file, publish.php, which you upload to your hosting just once. After that, the 🚀 Publish button puts the site online. You can also download the site as a .zip, or publish it through GitHub.

    Accessibility and SEO checked for you. Before each export, Atelier checks the site: images without a description, broken links, hard-to-read text (contrast), a form with no address… The 🔎 SEO window gives each page a score for Google and tells you what to improve. Atelier can also prepare the “Accessibility statement” page.

    Versions and a way back. Ctrl+Z undoes your last steps (up to 150). On top of that, Atelier keeps versions of the project: one automatically at the start of each working day, one before every risky operation, and the ones you save yourself. You can restore any of them at any time.

    A self-test. One command (Ctrl+K, then “Self-test”) replays Atelier’s main operations in your browser, checks that everything works and gives you a report to send if something is wrong. Your project is put back exactly as it was.

    Where to start

    1. Follow the getting-started tutorial: Getting started: the Kumakōgen tourist site in two pages. Step by step, you build a real site in three languages and come across everything Atelier can do.
    2. Keep the widget catalogue to hand: All the widgets. Every block and every one of its settings, explained simply.
    3. To go further, the chapters of the guide describe each feature in detail (contents).

    Good to know: the editor is designed for a screen at least 1024 pixels wide (a laptop is fine). To see the site as it looks on a phone, use the Mobile button at the top of the screen.

    A short glossary

    WordWhat it means
    PageA page of the site (the home page, the events, the contact page). It has its own title and address (/agenda/).
    SectionA wide horizontal band across the page. A page is a series of sections.
    RowA line of columns inside a section.
    ColumnA box in a row, where you place the widgets. On a phone, the columns move one below the other.
    WidgetA content block: heading, text, image, button, gallery, map, form… (the complete list).
    CanvasThe area in the middle of the screen, which shows the page as it will be published. This is where you click and type.
    InspectorThe right-hand panel: the settings of the selected element, in three tabs (Content, Style, Advanced).
    Left panelThe Widgets, Templates, Structure, Pages, Content and Site tabs.
    TemplateA ready-made section (a big welcome heading, an FAQ, a contact section…) that you add in one click.
    ComponentA block you have made reusable: it can appear on several pages and you only need to change it once.
    PresetA style saved under a name (“Primary button”) so you can reuse it on other elements.
    PaletteThe site’s colours, set in the Site panel. Changing the palette recolours the whole site.
    CollectionA list of items of the same kind (the events in a calendar, news articles, members). Each item has its own form and its own page.
    DraftA collection item that is not published yet: it does not appear on the site.
    Main languageThe language you write in first; the other languages are translations. A text that has not been translated is shown in the main language.
    AnchorA name given to a spot on the page (#acces) so that a link can take visitors straight there.
    ExportDownload the complete site as a .zip file, after a check.
    PublishPut the site online with your hosting provider.
    VersionA complete copy of the project, kept in your browser, which you can restore.
    LogThe list of everything the editor does (🐞 button): only useful for reporting a problem.
